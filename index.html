Headlines
The curiosity gap: why unfinished thoughts outperform complete ones

Your headline's job isn't to summarise — it's to create a gap between what the reader knows and what they want to know. The bigger that gap, the harder it is to scroll away. Here's how to engineer it deliberately.

The technique: Give them a taste of the destination without showing the route.
  • "The email subject line that got a 62% open rate (it's not what you think)" — you know what it's about, not why it worked.
  • "We fired our best customer. Here's what happened next." — the outcome is withheld.
  • "The word I removed from my sales page that doubled conversions." — you know action was taken, not the answer.

Common mistake: Curiosity without payoff is clickbait. The gap must be genuinely answered in the content.

The subject line formula that gets my emails a 62% open rate

Most subject lines are either too vague or too salesy. The formula I use balances specificity with intrigue — and it works consistently across niches.

The formula: [Specific outcome or number] + [unexpected twist or context]
  • "How I wrote 1 email that made $14k (in 200 words)"
  • "The LinkedIn post I almost didn't publish (it went viral)"
  • "Your homepage is losing you leads — here's the exact line"
Specificity creates believability. The twist creates intrigue. Together, they demand a click.

Persuasion
Why "you" is the most powerful word in any sales copy

Most brands write about themselves. The brands that convert write directly to the reader. One pronoun shift can transform a flat page into a conversation.

Test it yourself:
  • ❌ "Our platform helps businesses grow."
  • ✅ "You grow your business — we just make it faster."
  • ❌ "We offer personalised service to every client."
  • ✅ "You get a copywriter who learns your brand like it's theirs."
Count the "you" vs "we" ratio in your homepage. If "we" wins — rewrite it.

SEO Writing
Write for readers, optimise for Google — in that order

SEO copy that reads like SEO copy doesn't work. Here's how to integrate keywords naturally while writing content people actually want to read.

The priority order:
  • 1. Write for the human first — what's the point, the argument, the story?
  • 2. Identify where keywords fit naturally (headings, intro, conclusion)
  • 3. Check readability — Hemingway App, Grade 8 or below
  • 4. Add semantic keywords in subheadings
The rule: if your keyword integration sounds awkward out loud, Google can tell too.

Headlines
The 4 U's framework: how to audit every headline you write

Unique, Useful, Ultra-specific, Urgent. Any great headline scores on at least three of these. Learn to use this quick audit on every headline before publishing.

Score your headline 1–4 on each dimension:
  • Unique: Is this something only you could say?
  • Useful: Does it promise a clear benefit?
  • Ultra-specific: Numbers, names, timeframes beat vague claims.
  • Urgent: Is there a reason to read NOW rather than later?
Total score below 10? Rewrite it.
